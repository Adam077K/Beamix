## 1. The shift: SEO → AEO → GEO

- **Takeaway:** Three layers now, not one — and attention is migrating into the answer, where most brands aren't measuring.
- **Narrative:**

  Search hasn't died; it's fractured into three parallel universes, and they barely overlap. **SEO** is the old game: ranking blue links in Google, measured by position, clicks, and organic traffic. It still matters — organic is roughly 47% of all web traffic — but it's leaking clicks from the top down. **AEO**, Answer Engine Optimization, is about owning the zero-click surfaces *inside* Google: the featured snippet, the People Also Ask boxes, and the AI Overview that now sits on top of the results. **GEO**, Generative Engine Optimization, is the newest and the one that matters most to a software buyer in 2026: how often your brand gets mentioned, cited, and recommended inside the answers from ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Perplexity.

  The reason you can't treat these as one discipline is brutal and specific: Semrush found only about 2% overlap between Google's top-10 organic results and ChatGPT's citations. Ranking page one of Google now guarantees almost nothing inside the tools your buyers actually use. They are different games with different signals, different tools, and different winners.

  The strategic point: attention is migrating *into the answer*. The blue link was a doorway to your site. The AI answer is a verdict delivered before anyone reaches your door. If Imagen isn't measured and optimized across all three layers, you're optimizing for the layer that's shrinking and ignoring the two that are growing.

- **Key data:**
  - Organic search ≈ 46.98% of all web traffic, down ~3.65% in 2025 (SE Ranking, 2025).
  - Only ~2.1% overlap between Google top-10 organic and ChatGPT citations (Semrush via Digital Applied, early 2026).

## 4. How AI answer engines work

- **Takeaway:** Four engines, four personalities — but they all run the same pipeline, so a handful of levers move all of them.
- **Narrative:**

  Under the hood, every answer engine runs the same basic pipeline: it parses your question, fans it out into sub-questions, retrieves passages from the web (not whole pages — chunks), re-ranks them, lets the model write an answer, and then picks which sources to name. That's why answers are fluid: the model samples passages probabilistically, so the same question can produce a slightly different answer each time. But the four engines have distinct personalities, and Imagen's buyers use all of them.

  **ChatGPT** is GPT-5.5 Instant with Bing live search. It cites conservatively — the fewest sources of any engine — and it leans hard on what it already knows from training, which means it favors established, frequently-mentioned brands and consensus sources like Wikipedia. **Gemini** is Gemini 3.x wired into Google's full index, Knowledge Graph, and query fan-out; it cites heavily and rewards well-structured pages on your own domain. Roughly 38% of AI Overview citations come from outside the organic top-10 — *directional* — which means you can win Gemini without ranking #1. **Perplexity** is a RAG engine using Bing plus its own crawler; it's freshness-obsessed (most of its citations come from content under 30 days old), it's Reddit-heavy, and it reads llms.txt. **Claude** is Opus with native web search, commonly on a Brave Search backbone; it's the most selective citer, it honors robots.txt, and it rewards clear, structured, definitional content.

  The good news for you: even though they differ, the levers that move them are shared. Get mentioned across many independent sites. Put statistics, quotations, and citations into your content. Be structured and answer-first. Be fresh. Be present on Reddit and review platforms. Those few things move all four engines at once — which is exactly what makes a done-for-you program economical.

- **Key data:**
  - ChatGPT = GPT-5.5 (Instant) + Bing live search; ~2.6 citations/response — the lowest; leans on training-data priors (Profound/Erlin, 2026).
  - Gemini 3.x + Google index + Knowledge Graph + query fan-out; ~38% of AIO citations from outside organic top-10 — *directional* (Ahrefs/Digital Applied, 2026).
  - Perplexity = RAG, Bing + own crawler; ~82% of citations from content <30 days old; Reddit in 46.7% of answers; reads llms.txt (Neurobird/Perplexity, 2026).
  - Claude = Opus + native web_search (Brave backbone common); most selective citer; honors robots.txt (Anthropic subprocessor list, 2025).

## 5. How you actually get ranked: the 3-gate funnel

- **Takeaway:** Being in the answer is three gates, not one — Retrieved, Mentioned, Recommended-first — and each gate has its own fix.
- **Narrative:**

  "Getting ranked" in AI isn't a single event. It's three distinct gates, and most brands fail at a different one than they think. **Gate 1 — Retrieved:** your content has to enter the candidate pool at all. If the AI crawlers can't reach you — blocked in robots.txt, JavaScript-only content they can't render, not indexed — you're invisible before the race starts. This is table stakes and it's the single most common preventable cause of total AI invisibility. **Gate 2 — Mentioned:** being retrieved isn't being named. The model has to choose to put your brand in the sentence. That's driven by relevance, structure, E-E-A-T signals, and — heavily — how often you already appear across the web. **Gate 3 — Recommended-first:** being mentioned in passing is not being the top pick. Primacy is real: the first brand named carries the decision. Getting there takes corroboration across multiple independent sources, statistics density, freshness, and earned media.

  Now the signals that move each gate, because this is where the work is concrete. For **Retrieved**: crawler access, server-side rendering, indexing, schema. For **Mentioned**: answer-first structure (the model reads the first 40–60 words of each section), FAQ schema, and entity consistency — being described the same way everywhere. For **Recommended-first**: the peer-reviewed Princeton "GEO" study (KDD 2024) showed that adding statistics lifts visibility ~33% and adding quotations lifts it ~43%; earned media distribution produces a ~239% median citation lift; and being named across four or more independent sites gives roughly a 2.8x mention lift. None of this is mystical. It's a checklist with measured effect sizes.

  The reason I'm walking you through the gates is that it tells you *where* to spend. If Imagen is retrieved everywhere but only mentioned half the time, the fix is structure and statistics, not crawling. If you're mentioned but Aftershoot is always first, the fix is corroboration and earned media. The scorecard we're about to fill tells us which gate is yours.

- **Key data:**
  - Statistics Addition ≈ +33% visibility; Quotation Addition ≈ +43%; Cite Sources up to +40% (Aggarwal et al., Princeton "GEO," KDD 2024 — *peer-reviewed*).
  - Earned media distribution ≈ +239% median citation lift (Stacker, n=87 stories, Mar 2026).
  - Named across 4+ independent sites ≈ 2.8x mention lift; 64% of AI citations come from third-party sources, not your own site (Stacker/corroboration, 2026).
  - 44% of ChatGPT citations come from the first third of the content; model reads first 40–60 words per section (Profound/Erlin, 2026).
  - Blocking GPTBot/ClaudeBot/PerplexityBot in robots.txt = the most common preventable cause of total AI invisibility (multiple, 2026).
- **Suggested visual:** A vertical funnel with three narrowing stages — Retrieved → Mentioned → Recommended-first — and to the right of each stage, a small "levers" callout listing its 2–3 fixes (with the effect-size numbers next to Mentioned/Recommended).
- **Say this:** "Here's the model I use, and it's three gates. First, Retrieved — can the AI crawlers even reach you. Blocking them is the number-one own-goal. Second, Mentioned — being found isn't being named; that's structure, stats, and how often you show up across the web. Third, Recommended-first — being named once isn't being the top pick; the first brand mentioned usually wins, and getting there takes corroboration and earned media. The Princeton study put real numbers on it — adding statistics is about +33%, quotations about +43%, earned media a 239% lift. So this isn't vibes. It's a checklist with effect sizes. And the scorecard we're about to do tells us which gate is costing Imagen the most."

## 11. How we measure success: the GEO metric ladder + attribution

- **Takeaway:** We climb a ladder from AI visibility (leading) down to signups (lagging) — and we never report noise as if it were signal.
- **Narrative:**
